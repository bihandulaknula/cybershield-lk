LEARNING NOTE / 01

What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications and data from unauthorised access, disruption, damage or theft. It combines technology with people and processes.

A useful starting framework is the CIA triad: Confidentiality means information is accessible only to authorised people; Integrity means information remains accurate and trustworthy; and Availability means systems and information are accessible when needed.

For beginners, focus on understanding operating systems, networking, identity and access management, common attack patterns and safe defensive practices.

LEARNING NOTE / 02

Networking Basics

A network allows devices to exchange data. An IP address identifies a device or interface on a network, while DNS translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses.

Protocols define how communication happens. HTTP and HTTPS are used for web communication, DNS resolves names, and TCP provides reliable transport. Ports help a device direct network traffic to the correct service.

Practise by learning how to inspect your own network configuration and capture traffic in a lab environment you control.

LEARNING NOTE / 03

Recognising Phishing

Phishing is a social engineering technique that attempts to trick people into revealing information, opening harmful files or taking an unsafe action.

Warning signs can include unexpected urgency, unusual sender addresses, unexpected attachments, requests for passwords or payment, and links whose destination does not match the claimed organisation.

Verify requests through a trusted channel. Do not use contact details supplied in a suspicious message. Report suspected phishing through your organisation's approved process.
